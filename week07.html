/control/echo backgroundbackground · for non-physicists背景知识 · 写给非物理专业

Magnets bend charged particles: that is how momentum is measured.磁场使带电粒子拐弯，这就是测量动量的方法。

Background · The bending rule拐弯的规律

In a magnetic field B a charged particle moves on a circle of radius R, with p [GeV/c] = 0.3 B [T] R [m]. A stiffer, more energetic particle bends less. Measuring positions before and after a magnet gives the radius, hence the momentum.

带电粒子在磁场 B 中沿半径为 R 的圆运动，p [GeV/c] = 0.3 B [T] R [m]。能量越高、越“硬”的粒子拐得越少。在磁铁前后测量位置就能得到半径，进而得到动量。

Background · Does the Earth's field matter?地磁场有影响吗？

Barely, at detector scale. The geomagnetic field is about 50 µT, so a 4 GeV/c muon would curve with a radius of roughly 270 km. Across a 2 m telescope the deflection is negligible. The field does matter for the incoming cosmic-ray flux, which depends on latitude.

在探测器尺度上几乎没有影响。地磁场约 50 µT，4 GeV/c 的缪子曲率半径约 270 km。穿过 2 米长的望远镜，偏转可以忽略。但地磁场会影响入射宇宙线的通量，通量随纬度变化。

For muography projects用于缪子成像工程

Most muography telescopes have no magnet, so the momentum of each muon is unknown. That is a key uncertainty: low-momentum muons scatter more and blur the image. Some detectors add a lead or iron absorber layer to reject soft muons. Simulating with and without such a layer is a typical design study.

大多数缪子成像望远镜没有磁铁，所以每个缪子的动量未知。这是一个关键的不确定因素：低动量缪子散射更大，会使图像模糊。有些探测器加一层铅或铁吸收体来剔除软缪子。有无这层吸收体的对比模拟是一项典型的设计研究。

From "it passed through" to "it passed through carrying this much momentum".从“它穿过了”到“它带着这么多动量穿过了”。

Theorem 7.1 · The momentum–curvature relation动量—曲率关系

A particle of charge q and transverse momentum pT in a uniform field B follows a circle of radius R with pT = |q| B R, or pT [GeV/c] = 0.299792458 |q/e| B [T] R [m]. pT equals p only when the motion is perpendicular to the field; otherwise pT = p sin α. A stronger field and a longer lever arm improve resolution, at the cost of a bigger magnet.

电荷为 q、横动量为 pT 的粒子在均匀磁场 B 中沿半径为 R 的圆周运动，pT = |q| B R，即 pT [GeV/c] = 0.299792458 |q/e| B [T] R [m]。只有运动垂直于磁场时 pT 才等于 p；否则 pT = p sin α。更强的磁场和更长的杠杆臂能提高分辨率，代价是更大的磁铁。

For tracker–magnet–tracker the useful form follows from the same circle. Inside a field of length L along z, sin θ grows linearly with z at rate 1/R, so the two trackers measure

对“径迹室—磁铁—径迹室”布局，有用的形式来自同一个圆：在沿 z 长为 L 的磁场中，sin θ 随 z 以 1/R 的速率线性增长，所以两组径迹室测得

sin θout − sin θin = 0.2998 B Lp (7.1)

Fit one straight line through each tracker, take the change in slope, and Eq. (7.1) gives p with no assumption about where the hits sit. For 10 GeV/c in 1.5 T × 1 m the deflection is 45 mrad.

在每组径迹室里各拟合一条直线，取斜率的变化，式 (7.1) 就给出 p，无需假设测点位于何处。10 GeV/c 在 1.5 T × 1 m 中的偏转为 45 mrad。

Researcher's notebook · truth, measurement, reconstruction研究笔记 · 真相、测量、重建

The generator knows ptrue. The tracker measures positions. The algorithm infers prec. A good study keeps the three apart and asks how resolution, alignment, multiple scattering and field knowledge move prec away from ptrue.

产生器知道 ptrue，径迹室测量位置，算法推断 prec。好的研究把三者分开，并追问分辨率、准直、多次散射和磁场知识如何让 prec 偏离 ptrue。

Intuition · a bent ruler直觉 · 弯曲的尺子

The stronger the field or the lower the momentum, the more the track bends and the easier the bend is to measure. A very stiff track is nearly straight, its radius heads to infinity, and position errors are amplified. High-energy experiments need stronger fields or longer lever arms for that reason.

磁场越强或动量越低，径迹弯得越厉害，弯曲就越容易测准。很“硬”的径迹几乎是直的，半径趋于无穷，位置误差被放大。正因如此，高能实验需要更强的磁场或更长的杠杆臂。

7.2 From an ideal field to a measured field map7.2 从理想磁场到实测场图

Real magnets have fringe fields, coil imperfections and gradients. A 1D map B(z) suits a symmetric field; a 3D map is needed when transverse structure matters. The map must state its coordinates, units, interpolation, coverage and what happens outside it. Never silently replace a measured map with a uniform field: a 1% variation matters for a 10⁻³ observable. Step 5 measures exactly this.

真实磁铁有边缘场、线圈缺陷和梯度。对称磁场适合用一维场图 B(z)；横向结构重要时需要三维场图。场图必须写明坐标约定、单位、插值方法、覆盖范围以及范围外的处理。绝不要悄悄用均匀场代替实测场图：对 10⁻³ 量级的可观测量，1% 的变化就很重要。第 5 步测量的正是这一点。

TOY MODEL · approximations近似

Same layout as the week07 app. Exact circular arc in a uniform field; straight lines outside. Multiple scattering is a Gaussian kick at each plane from the Highland formula (PDG, β = 1), plus one kick per air gap. No energy loss, no δ-rays, no fringe field. The analytic line adds the measurement term (√2 × σx/√Σ(z − z̄)² over the deflection) and the scattering term in quadrature. The σ shown is the half-width of the central 68%. Below about 2 GeV the toy is up to 2× more optimistic than Geant4, which also has energy-loss fluctuations and full scattering tails.

布局与 week07 程序相同。均匀磁场中为精确圆弧，场外为直线。多次散射为每个平面上的高斯角度冲击（Highland 公式，PDG，β = 1），每段空气间隙再加一次。没有能量损失、δ 电子和边缘场。解析线把测量项（√2 × σx/√Σ(z − z̄)² 除以偏转角）与散射项平方相加。显示的 σ 是中心 68% 的半宽。约 2 GeV 以下，玩具模型比 Geant4 乐观多达 2 倍，因为 Geant4 还包含能损涨落和完整的散射尾巴。

// (to 0.1%), but the field now leaks into the trackers. Units: Geant4 internal.
class FringeField : public G4MagneticField
{
  public:
    FringeField(G4double b0 = 1.5 * tesla, G4double halfLength = 0.5 * m, G4double edge = 5. * cm)
      : fB0(b0), fHalf(halfLength), fEdge(edge) {}

    void GetFieldValue(const G4double point[4], G4double* field) const override
    {
      const G4double z = point[2];
      field[0] = 0.;
      field[1] = fB0 / (1. + std::exp((std::abs(z) - fHalf) / fEdge));
      field[2] = 0.;
    }

  private:

};

    """Lab 7.1 analysis: reconstruct momentum from the six tracker planes.

Truth -> measurement -> reconstruction, kept apart:
  truth          x_i, z_i where the primary entered plane i (Geant4)        [CSV]
  measurement    x_i smeared by the plane resolution sigma_x (here, in Python)
  reconstruction a straight line through each tracker; the change of slope across
                 the dipole gives p:   sin(theta_out) - sin(theta_in) = 0.2998 B L / p

Also runs a global quadratic fit through all six hits on the same events, to show its bias
for a tracker-magnet-tracker layout (that model assumes all hits sit inside the field).

    python3 reconstruct.py            # needs numpy only
"""
import glob
import math

import numpy as np

B_T, L_M = 1.5, 1.0          # field [T] and magnet length [m], as in DetectorConstruction
SIGMA_X_MM = 0.050           # plane resolution: 50 um
K = 0.299792458              # GeV / (T m)
rng = np.random.default_rng(7)


def load(path):
    data = np.loadtxt(path, delimiter=",", comments="#")
    return data[:, 0], data[:, 2:8] / 1000.0, data[:, 8:14] / 1000.0   # p [GeV], x [m], z [m]


def line_slope(z, x):
    """Least-squares slope dx/dz for each event (rows) through 3 points."""
    zm, xm = z.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True), x.mean(axis=1, keepdims=True)
    return ((z - zm) * (x - xm)).sum(axis=1) / ((z - zm) ** 2).sum(axis=1)


def p_two_lines(z, x):
    t_in, t_out = line_slope(z[:, :3], x[:, :3]), line_slope(z[:, 3:], x[:, 3:])
    s_in, s_out = t_in / np.sqrt(1 + t_in ** 2), t_out / np.sqrt(1 + t_out ** 2)
    return K * B_T * L_M / np.abs(s_out - s_in)


def p_quadratic(z, x):
    """Global quadratic x(z) = a z^2 + b z + c through all six points (biased here)."""
    out = np.empty(len(z))
    for i in range(len(z)):
        a = np.polyfit(z[i], x[i], 2)[0]
        out[i] = K * B_T / abs(2 * a)
    return out


def core_width(r):
    """Half-width of the central 68% of r: the Gaussian core, insensitive to a few
    scattering tail events that dominate np.std on 1000 events."""
    lo, hi = np.percentile(r, [15.865, 84.135])
    return 0.5 * (hi - lo)


print(f"{'file':<28}{'p_true':>8}{'bias(2 lines)':>15}{'sigma_p/p':>11}{'core68':>8}{'bias(quad)':>12}")
for path in sorted(glob.glob("tracks_*GeV_nt_tracks.csv")):
    p_true, x, z = load(path)
    x_meas = x + rng.normal(0.0, SIGMA_X_MM / 1000.0, x.shape)       # measurement
    r2 = (p_two_lines(z, x_meas) - p_true) / p_true
    rq = (p_quadratic(z, x_meas) - p_true) / p_true
    print(f"{path:<28}{p_true.mean():8.3f}{np.median(r2):+15.4f}{np.std(r2):11.4f}{core_width(r2):8.4f}{np.median(rq):+12.3f}")

    highland.pyANALYSIS
    """Compare the simulated scattering angle in steel with the Highland formula.

    python3 highland.py          reads slab_<t>cm_<E>GeV.root from slab_scan.mac

The upper two planes give the incoming slope, the lower two the outgoing slope.
The plane-projected deflection is theta_x = atan(sx_out) - atan(sx_in) (same for y).
PDG RPP 2024 Eq. 34.16 (Highland, as refined by Lynch and Dahl):
    theta0 = 13.6 MeV / (beta c p) * z * sqrt(x/X0) * [1 + 0.038 ln(x z^2 / (X0 beta^2))]
theta0 is the width of the CENTRAL 98% of the projected-angle distribution, so we
compare it with the r.m.s. of the central 98% of the simulated angles, not the
full r.m.s. (single large-angle scatters make the full r.m.s. larger).
"""
import numpy as np
import uproot

M_MU = 0.1056584       # GeV
X0_FE = 1.757          # cm, PDG
Z_PLANE = np.array([60.0, 40.0, -40.0, -60.0])   # cm, DetectorConstruction::kPlaneZ


def highland(t_cm, ekin):
    E = ekin + M_MU
    p = np.sqrt(E * E - M_MU * M_MU)
    beta = p / E
    x = t_cm / X0_FE
    return 13.6e-3 / (beta * p) * np.sqrt(x) * (1 + 0.038 * np.log(x / beta**2))   # rad


def central98_rms(a):
    lo, hi = np.percentile(a, [1, 99])
    a = a[(a >= lo) & (a <= hi)]
    return a.std()


def angles(t):
    sx_in = (t["x0"] - t["x1"]) / (Z_PLANE[0] - Z_PLANE[1])
    sy_in = (t["y0"] - t["y1"]) / (Z_PLANE[0] - Z_PLANE[1])
    sx_out = (t["x2"] - t["x3"]) / (Z_PLANE[2] - Z_PLANE[3])
    sy_out = (t["y2"] - t["y3"]) / (Z_PLANE[2] - Z_PLANE[3])
    return np.concatenate([np.arctan(sx_out) - np.arctan(sx_in), np.arctan(sy_out) - np.arctan(sy_in)])


print(f"{'steel':>6} {'E_kin':>6} {'muons':>6} {'sim theta0':>11} {'Highland':>9} {'sim/Highland':>13}")
for t_cm in (1, 2, 5, 10):
    for e in (1, 3, 10):
        tree = uproot.open(f"slab_{t_cm}cm_{e}GeV.root")["trk"].arrays(library="np")
        th = angles(tree)
        sim = central98_rms(th)
        hl = highland(t_cm, e)
        err = sim / np.sqrt(2 * len(th))   # approximate statistical error of a width
        print(f"{t_cm:>4} cm {e:>4} GeV {len(tree['x0']):>6} {1e3 * sim:>7.2f} mrad {1e3 * hl:>6.2f} mrad "
              f"{sim / hl:>8.3f} +/- {err / hl:.3f}")
    poca.pyANALYSIS
    """A first scattering-muography image: Point of Closest Approach (PoCA).

    python3 poca.py poca.root

For each muon: the incoming line (planes 0, 1) and the outgoing line (planes 2, 3)
are straight lines in 3D. If the muon scattered once, the two lines meet where it
scattered. PoCA puts the whole deflection at the point where the two lines come
closest, and weights it by the squared scattering angle. Dense, high-Z material
lights up. Real muography adds detector resolution, a momentum estimate and
iterative methods (e.g. maximum likelihood); PoCA is the 10-line baseline.
"""
import sys

import matplotlib
matplotlib.use("Agg")
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
import uproot

Z = np.array([60.0, 40.0, -40.0, -60.0])   # cm, plane positions
t = uproot.open(sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else "poca.root")["trk"].arrays(library="np")

P = [np.stack([t[f"x{i}"], t[f"y{i}"], np.full_like(t["x0"], Z[i])], axis=1) for i in range(4)]
a0, u = P[1], P[0] - P[1]                 # incoming line: point on plane 1, direction
b0, v = P[2], P[2] - P[3]                 # outgoing line: point on plane 2, direction
u /= np.linalg.norm(u, axis=1)[:, None]
v /= np.linalg.norm(v, axis=1)[:, None]
theta = np.arccos(np.clip(np.sum(u * v, axis=1), -1, 1))     # 3D scattering angle

# closest points of the two lines: minimise |a0 + s u - (b0 + r v)|
w = a0 - b0
uv = np.sum(u * v, axis=1)
den = np.maximum(1 - uv**2, 1e-12)
s = (uv * np.sum(w * v, axis=1) - np.sum(w * u, axis=1)) / den
r = (np.sum(w * v, axis=1) - uv * np.sum(w * u, axis=1)) / den
poca = 0.5 * ((a0 + s[:, None] * u) + (b0 + r[:, None] * v))

sel = (theta > 2e-3) & (np.abs(poca[:, 2]) < 35)               # scattered, between the trackers
inside = (np.abs(poca[:, 0]) < 10) & (np.abs(poca[:, 1]) < 10) & (np.abs(poca[:, 2]) < 10)
big = theta > 20e-3
print(f"{len(theta)} muons; median scattering angle {1e3 * np.median(theta):.1f} mrad")
print(f"PoCA inside the cube: {inside.sum()} muons; {np.sum(inside & big)} of them "
      f"({100 * np.mean(big[inside]):.0f}%) scattered by more than 20 mrad")
print(f"PoCA outside the cube: {np.sum(~inside)} muons; {np.sum(~inside & big)} "
      f"({100 * np.mean(big[~inside]):.1f}%) scattered by more than 20 mrad")

edges = np.linspace(-40, 40, 41)

cnt, _, _ = np.histogram2d(poca[sel, 0], poca[sel, 1], bins=[edges, edges])
