/run/initializethe problem

Copying an example gets a simulation running. It does not tell you if it is right.

Most physicists learn Geant4 alone, by editing exampleB1 until something compiles. The output looks plausible, and nobody checks it against physics. This course is built around the check.

// what most people do
cp -r examples/basic/B1 mySim
cmake .. && make
./exampleB1 run1.mac
// histogram looks fine?
// ship it

Runs. Unknown whether it is right.

// what you will do
build geometry, verify volumes
choose physics list, justify cuts
score Edep, compare to Bethe–Bloch
// mean dE/dx agrees with PDG within errors
// result you can defend

Runs, and you know why the numbers are right.

/control/executethe method

Five stages, from seeing a track to trusting a number.

StageYou learn to阶段
1 · See itBuild geometry and materials, and draw your first tracks观测
2 · Measure itScore energy, record hits with a trigger, and write ntuples you read in Python度量
3 · Understand itChoose physics lists and production cuts, and know what they change理解
4 · Build itWrite a complete detector application with sensitive detectors建造
5 · Trust itDebug systematically, validate against reference data, and quote an uncertainty信任

/run/beamOn 12what you build

Twelve events. Each one ends with working code.

WeeksProject
01–02A two-layer muon telescope in Python, then your first Geant4 application and its muon tracks
03–05Geometry and materials, energy loss against the Bethe formula, hits and a coincidence trigger
06–08Physics lists and production cuts, momentum from a magnetic field, a multithreaded analysis chain
09–10musrSim and a transverse-field μSR experiment (optional for the muography track)
11–12Debugging and validation against PDG, then a capstone proposal you defend
