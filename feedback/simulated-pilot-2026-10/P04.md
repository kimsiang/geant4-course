# P04 · Marco Bellini: pilot feedback (physics track, EN)

**Who I am.** First-year experimental PhD student (Italy/PSI), starting on the muon EDM. Strong in C++17 and ROOT. I have run B1 and B4 but have never written a Geant4 application from scratch. I have about 4 h a week and no patience for material I already know.

**What I did.** I skimmed the course home, the primer's Geant4 section, Weeks 1–4 and Week 12. I read Weeks 5–11 completely, including every code file. I compiled Weeks 5, 7, 8 and 10 against Geant4 11.4 and ran them.
- Weeks 5, 7 and 8 reproduced the expected output digit for digit: trigger.mac, reconstruct.py, the MT daq.mac, read_muons.py and parity.py.
- Week 10 at 60k muons gave A0 = 0.29 ± 0.02 and f = 1.3579 ± 0.0047 MHz, consistent with the page.
- The harness itself caused two hiccups: a link error (`anHCAllocator_G4MT_TLS_`, because the pybind library lacks G4digits_hits) and a shared tmp directory. Neither is a course bug.

## What is genuinely good

The code is modern and clean. It uses G4RunManagerFactory, a lazy hits-collection ID, ConstructSDandField for fields and SDs, G4AnalysisManager with ntuple merging, G4Accumulable, and G4GenericMessenger for pre-init parameters. The physics numbers I checked are right:
- 45 mrad at 10 GeV/c
- p = 1.101 GeV/c for T = 1 GeV
- about 0.5 MeV lost before the magnet
- 135.5388 MHz/T and the precession direction (S × B sends −z to −y)
- the cone formula for A0
- 2τR₁R₂
- Birks at minimum ionisation, 0.976
- a pion interaction length of 19.9 cm in Pb

Two parts stand out:
- **Week 10.** The `G4SpinDecayPhysics` point is exactly the trap that catches muon people. I checked that it has physics type 0, so `RegisterPhysics` after `G4DecayPhysics` really does replace "Decay". The no-spin control with a "converged" fit is excellent teaching.
- **Week 11.** The path-averaged reference and the truncated-mean trap are the best validation lesson I have seen. I also confirmed with the pre-built fault-1 binary that a vertex outside the world is tracked without complaint.

The race the course found in `G4DecayWithSpin` (Course-W12-b) is real. Lines ~98 and ~174 of the 11.4 source write `parent_polarization` into a decay table that all threads share. That is a finding worth reporting to the Geant4 developers.

## What would embarrass the course in front of Geant4 developers

1. **"Our sandbox."** Weeks 2, 3, 6, 9 and 12 say the authors could not run things. Most expected outputs come from an undistributed "course test physics list", while the code I am given uses FTFP_BERT. Week 6, the physics-list week, shows only a *prediction* for the FTFP_BERT run. Meanwhile the home page promises "Expected output from real Geant4 11.4 runs". Rerun everything with FTFP_BERT and the datasets, and delete every "sandbox" sentence.
2. **musrSim was never built or run** (Week 9, and the musrSim part of Week 10). This is a course by the musrsim-sms authors, and the only environment recipe is a CentOS 7 LCG_101 CVMFS view. I have neither. Ship a container with Geant4 10.7, ROOT and musrSim prebuilt, and replace every output marked "from the source, not from a run".
3. **The `G4DecayWithSpin` race is presented as a reproducibility nuisance.** It is a data race on the spin used to generate the positron, so it is a correctness issue, however rare. File a bug report, cite it, and make the μSR reference app single-threaded by default.

## Concrete bugs (verified)

- **week09 · production.sh, line 10.** `grep -q … || echo … | cat - 201_prod.mac > tmp && mv tmp 201_prod.mac` parses as `(grep || pipe) && mv`. In the normal case grep succeeds, `tmp` never exists, and `set -e` kills the script before the job loop. I reproduced this in bash.
- **week10 · src/CounterSD.cc.** There is no energy threshold, which breaks Week 5's own rule.
  - In my run, 0.3% (U) and 0.8% (D) of counter hits have `det_time_start` ≈ 0.8 ns, microseconds before the decay, with a median deposit of 30 keV: δ-rays from the incoming muon.
  - In `--measured` mode these become t = 0 positrons. That is why f shifts by almost 1σ between the truth and measured fits on the same events (1.3573 → 1.3556 on the page; 1.3579 → 1.3549 in my run).
  - Fix: apply a threshold per counter and take the first time above it.
- **week06 · quiz Q5.** "Below about 10 µm in plastic … the e⁻ threshold no longer changes" is wrong for electrons, according to the course's own `cut_thresholds_g4_11.4.json`: 14.5 keV at 10 µm, 1.7 keV at 3 µm, and the 990 eV floor only at 1 µm. The 990 eV is the default low edge of the production-threshold table (`/cuts/setLowEdge`), not "Geant4 never produces secondaries below it".
- **week07 · integrator check.** "0.0031 against 0.0040 … because the run produces different random scattering" is not right. `np.std` on 1000 events is driven by the tails. On your own CSV files the central-68% half-width at 1 GeV is 0.0029, and five events sit beyond 5 widths. The toy uses the 68% half-width, so the lab should too.
- **week10 · Lab 10.** "The few nanoseconds each muon spends in flight" cannot bias f. A constant time offset moves φ, not f.
