# P02 · Dr. Aiko Tanaka: volcanology postdoc, muography track, EN mode

**About me.** I am a volcanology postdoc in Japan. I model volcano density from gravity and seismic data. I have read the Sakurajima muography papers and the nuclear-emulsion work. Now I want to simulate a telescope on a volcano flank, with the terrain taken from a DEM and the open-sky flux at zenith angles of 70–90°. My Python and statistics are strong, I am comfortable on Linux (MacBook M2 plus a server), my C++ is basic, and my particle physics is weak. I have about 5 h a week.

What I did: I read the primer and Weeks 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 11 and 12 completely. I skimmed Weeks 2, 5 and 8, and only glanced at 9 and 10, which are optional on my track. I recomputed every number I could check. I ran Week 1 and compiled the Week 3, 4 and 7 applications against Geant4 11.4.

## First impressions

The course home and the primer are excellent at welcoming non-physicists. "No particle physics needed: civil engineers and geologists are welcome", the muography track, and the table that maps project steps to weeks told me exactly where to start. The predict → simulate → compare rhythm is the best I have seen in a software course. Every expected output comes from a real run, and every toy model lists its approximations. As a person who checks numbers for a living, I trusted the pages quickly.

I checked about fifteen numbers and almost all are right:
- 1 cm⁻² min⁻¹ ≈ 167 s⁻¹ m⁻²
- γ = 37.8 and a 25 km decay length at 4 GeV
- the standard-rock range table and its conversions to metres and m.w.e.
- 0.89° of scattering for 3 GeV in 1 m of rock (Highland, X₀ = 26.5 g/cm²)
- the βγ values and T_max = 4.8 GeV at 10 GeV
- 45 mrad at 10 GeV/c, and R ≈ 270 km in the geomagnetic field
- the Week 1 reference integrals (0.04901, 0.1336, 0.1702), which I reproduced independently with SciPy

Week 1 printed the expected digits exactly.

## The problem for my project

The muography track promises "a void above a tunnel, an overburden or a dam density", and on the home page it promises Geant4 as "the standard tool for simulating it before a detector goes into the field". Yet the two hardest inputs of a volcano simulation are never taught.

1. **The sky at large zenith angles.** Primer · 2: "angular distribution ∝ cos²θ … around 3 GeV". That is fine for overhead muons, but cos²θ goes to zero at horizontal, and every line of sight toward my volcano lies at θ ≈ 75–88°. There, only muons above tens of GeV survive, and the energy–angle correlation dominates. The page should name Gaisser's formula with the Earth-curvature correction, Reyna/Bugaev, Tang et al., or measured horizontal spectra, and it should mention altitude and geomagnetic effects. The primer's table lists "CRY, EcoMug or a parameterisation in practice" for Weeks 1, 2 and 5. But the only Geant4 generator in the course (Week 5) is fixed at 4 GeV with a cos²θ shape. A geologist who follows the primer literally will badly underestimate near-horizontal rates.
2. **Terrain from a DEM.** Week 3 · Background gives it one sentence: "terrain from a digital elevation model … often imported through CAD/GDML". There is no G4TessellatedSolid, no conversion from DEM to GDML, no coordinate frame, and no check of rock length along lines of sight. This is exactly where I need hand-holding, because my C++ is basic.
3. **Acceptance.** Week 1's table says AΩ [m² sr] belongs to "wk 12 projects", but Week 12 never computes it. The same page also contradicts itself. Its muography note says "The quantity you compute this week is a telescope's acceptance", while its own common error MC0003 says that calling ε the acceptance is a mistake. The course also only ever shows horizontal planes, and volcano telescopes stand vertical, facing the flank.
4. **Feasibility.** Week 12 uses ΔN/√N. For volcanoes, the limits are flux-model systematics and the low-energy background near horizontal. Neither is mentioned anywhere, not even in Primer · 4. The planner also computes CPU time, when what I need is exposure time in days.

## Other concrete issues
- **week06 · Practice quiz Q5** is wrong by the course's own data. It says "Below about 10 µm in plastic, lowering the cut no longer changes the e⁻ threshold", but cut_thresholds_g4_11.4.json gives 14.5 keV at 10 µm, and the threshold only reaches 990 eV at 1 µm.
- **primer · 3 Muons in rock:** "in standard rock the two are equal near 500 GeV". As far as I know, PDG gives a muon critical energy of about 693 GeV for standard rock, and 500 GeV is the a/b approximation. Please check, and say which one you mean.
- **week04 · Lab 4, RunAction.cc:** the comment says "~28 MeV at 100 MeV", but Course-W04-a and the output give about 24 MeV. The lab also stops at 10 GeV in 10 cm of plastic. A rock extension up to 1 TeV, showing range straggling, would connect it to muography.
- **week03 · steps_summary.py:** the docstring uses "run100.mac", which does not exist. The script also crashes with ZeroDivisionError when the log has no events, which happened to me when my run aborted.
- **week12 · repro_check.sh** only reads a headline line matching `^f +=` (μSR). For a muography headline it fails with ValueError.
- **week07 · 7.1:** the heading reads "## 8.2 From an ideal field…".
- **week11 · Lab 11.1:** "instructor builds the fault lab". Self-paced, I am the instructor, and the sed lines reveal every fault.
- **Setup:** there are no install instructions for Geant4 11.4 with its data sets, on any platform. Without the data sets the apps abort (G4ENSDFSTATEDATA). For non-physicists this is the most likely place to give up.
- **Support:** no help channel is named anywhere.
