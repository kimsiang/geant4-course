# P10 · Dr. Helen Brooks: purchase evaluation, Geant4 2.0

*Principal geophysicist, mining services, Perth. Ex-ATLAS postdoc; my last serious Geant4 was 9.x. I am deciding whether to buy 8 seats for junior geophysicists. I read the home page, the primer and Weeks 1, 4, 6, 7 and 11 in full. I skimmed Weeks 2, 3, 5, 8 and 12, and gave 9 and 10 only a glance because they are optional on the muography track.*

## Verdict

**NPS 6.** The physics is better than most paid courses I have seen. The product around it is not ready for me to buy. If the licence, support and a muography lab arrive, I would raise this to 8–9.

## What I checked, and what held

I checked every number I could against PDG and against my own arithmetic.

**Correct:**
- Muon constants: τ = 2.197 µs, m = 105.7 MeV.
- Flux: 1 cm⁻² min⁻¹ is about 167 m⁻² s⁻¹. The vertical intensity of 70 m⁻² s⁻¹ sr⁻¹ is quoted with the right "recent data lower" caveat.
- Energy and decay length: ⟨E⟩ ≈ 4 GeV, which gives γ ≈ 38 and a decay length of 25 km. cos²θ is quoted near 3 GeV.
- Standard rock: 2.65 g/cm³ and X₀ = 26.5 g/cm². Every row of the range table is consistent (553 g/cm² → 2.1 m … 245 300 → 926 m).
- Highland: 0.89° for 3 GeV/c through 1 m of rock.
- Bethe formula inputs: K = 0.307075, and for PVT I = 64.7 eV and Z/A = 0.54141. The βγ values and Tmax ≈ 4.8 GeV at 10 GeV are right.
- Spectrometer: 45 mrad at 10 GeV/c in 1.5 T·m, and the 0.44 m sideways bend at 1 GeV.
- The +61% bias of a global quadratic fit. I derived it independently: the fitted curvature is 0.62 of the true one.
- The Earth-field radius of 267 km.

**Reproduced:**
- I re-ran the Week 1 script and every digit matched (NumPy 2.5.3). My own integrals give 0.04901, 0.1336 and 0.1702, as the page says.
- `validate_dedx.py` on the author's Week 4 files reproduced the page.
- week04, week06 and week07 compile against 11.4 with FTFP_BERT.

The TOY MODEL labels and "what is real and what is a toy" boxes are the most honest I have seen in teaching material.

## Where a sceptic stops nodding

1. **week11 · 11.3 / Lab 11.2: "All five agree within 0.4σ."** This is the headline of the validation week, and it is the weakest statistics in the course.
   - The stat-only pulls are −2.0, −1.0, −2.2, −1.0 and −0.2. All have the same sign, χ² = 10.8/5 and p = 0.06.
   - The "0.1–0.4σ" values appear only after adding an assumed 1% reference error that PDG does not quote. With it, χ² = 0.28/5. Agreement that good happens 0.2% of the time, so the error model is inflated.
   - That 1% (mostly the I-value) is fully correlated across energies, so it must not be added to each point independently.
   - A validation week should *teach* the too-good χ² as a warning sign. Instead the course has put it on the hero card ("≤ 0.4σ at 5 energies") and in the README.

2. **week07 · Check the integrator.** "0.0031 against 0.0040 … different random scattering." The same page says σ is known to ±3%. A 25% change is not a fluctuation: `np.std` on 1000 events is being pulled around by a few tail events. Meanwhile the toy uses a central-68% width. Use one robust width with a bootstrap error.

3. **week07 · Lab 7.2: "15σ at 1 GeV and 5σ at 20 GeV."** The bias is a median, and the shift is a difference between two runs. With the right error the shift is about 9σ at 1 GeV. At 20 GeV it is 2–3σ, depending on which baseline you pick, and the page mixes baselines.

4. **week04 · "agree with the formula to 0.5%."** At 200 MeV that is about 5σ. Week 1 drills "quote in σ", then Week 4 switches to percent and never names the formula's own accuracy as the systematic.

5. **week11 · fault-6 row: "0.0050 ± 0.0050".** That is k = 1 of 200, using the binomial σ that Week 1 (MC0004) says fails at the boundary. It is also 2.8σ below main, which is right at the page's own L0 threshold. The "nothing broken" verdict is probably right but is argued from one column.

6. **week06 · Quiz Q5: "Below about 10 µm in plastic … no longer changes the e⁻ threshold."** The course's own threshold table says 14.5 keV at 10 µm and 1.74 keV at 3 µm. The floor arrives at 1 µm. The quiz key is teaching something false.

7. **week06 · Run A is a "PREDICTION, not run".** The week's key comparison was never executed. More generally, every "real Geant4 11.4" output came from a dataset-free test list. The home FAQ's "Are the numbers real? Yes" should say that.

8. **week11 · Course-W11-a: "Geant4 11.4 does not stop it."** I may be out of date, but G4SteppingManager has a fatal Tracking0010, "Primary vertex outside of the world!" (it is even a quiz distractor). Show the log. If the claim is true it belongs in a Geant4 bug report.

**Smaller items:**
- week01 · Background calls ε "a telescope's acceptance", which the same page forbids two sections later.
- week04 · Quiz Q1: "10–15%" should be 8% at entry.
- week04 · `RunAction.cc` comment: "~28 MeV" should be 24.
- week07 · A section is numbered "8.2".
- primer · "equal near 500 GeV": PDG also gives Eμc = 693 GeV for standard rock, so say which definition.
- primer · "0.9°" is the projected θ₀.
- index / primer · the toy spectrum caption should also say the real spectrum steepens above ~100 GeV, which is exactly the deep-rock regime.
- week12 · "ΔN/√N": at month-long exposures, the flux-model and density systematics dominate. My juniors would over-promise significance to clients with that formula.

## As a product for a company

**Content fit.** The muography track never puts a realistic sky through real rock. The primer promises "CRY, EcoMug or a parameterisation in practice", and no week delivers it. There is no layered overburden, no flux-ratio map and no scattering muography. That is the work I would be paying for. Weeks 9–10 (musrSim, μSR) are excellent for physicists and irrelevant to us.

**Setup.** My juniors use locked-down Windows laptops. The README assumes an existing Geant4 11.4 with datasets. It has no build guide, no WSL page, and none of the container that the landing page promises.

**Landing page vs course.** These contradict each other:
- time: "about five hours a week" vs "2–3 h";
- prerequisites: "basic C++: classes and pointers" vs "Week 1 starts in Python";
- Weeks 9–10 are described as "a multi-layer muon telescope, as used in muography", but they are musrSim;
- "MPV within 2%" vs labs that compare means.

I would not let a vendor get away with that in a tender.

**Evidence of competence.** Progress lives in localStorage, which is good for privacy. But I cannot export it to a training record. There is no certificate, and nobody grades the capstone for a self-paced learner. Week 11's fault lab is spoiled for self-paced students because `make_faults.sh` and its comments sit on the student page.

**Support.** There is none listed: no forum, no email and no office hours. Week 1 even tells students to post in a "course group" that doesn't exist. AWST is the same time zone as Shanghai, so live support would actually be easy for us.

**Licence and privacy.**
- "Licence: not yet chosen … all rights reserved." That is a blocker for procurement.
- I also need to know who owns capstone code that may embed client site data.
- The waitlist form posts to a third-party form service with no privacy note.
- "Nothing is sent anywhere" ignores Google Fonts and the CDNs.

## What I need before paying

1. A written licence, including a corporate 8+ seat option. It should cover IP in capstone work and include a privacy notice.
2. A named support channel with response times and office hours.
3. A container with Geant4 11.4, the datasets and the Python stack, plus a WSL page.
4. Expected outputs regenerated with FTFP_BERT and full datasets, and Week 6 Run A actually run.
5. A muography lab with a realistic generator, layered rock, a void and an exposure estimate, validated against an open-sky rate.
6. The Week 11 headline fixed to stat-only pulls and χ², with the 1% treated as a correlated nuisance.
7. An externally graded capstone, a certificate, and a progress export.

**Price I would accept:**
- self-paced: A$300–450 per seat;
- with code review and office hours: A$900–1,200 per seat;
- about US$4–5k for 8 supported seats once items 1–4 exist.

**The one change I want most:** a muography capstone lab sold under a real licence with real support. The physics teaching is already good enough; the course has to sell to companies as well.
