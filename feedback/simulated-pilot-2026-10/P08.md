# P08 · Daniel Lim: senior C++ engineer, physics track

**NPS 7/10.** The code is the best part of this course and the environment setup is the worst part. I read every week's lesson; I read weeks 2–8 and 10 completely and skimmed weeks 9, 11 and 12. I compiled weeks 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 10 against Geant4 11.4 and ran every macro and script whose output the pages quote. For weeks 9 and 11 I ran the Python and shell scripts.

## What I verified

- **All eight apps build first time.** With `-Wall -Wextra -Wshadow` the only warnings are local variables named `s` and `m` shadowing Geant4's `second` and `metre`.
- **The expected outputs are real.** Using the test physics list, weeks 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8 and 11 reproduced the page's numbers to the last printed digit. That includes the two-thread merged ntuple in Week 8 (322 triggers), the six-material step table in Week 3 and the Bethe table in Week 4. Week 2's pion run gave 211 decays against 205 ± 13. Week 10's 10 mT fit came out within the stated errors.
- **Consistency is good.** Every week uses the same `main()` skeleton and the same CMake template. Macros and scripts are copied into `build/`. Knobs are set through `G4GenericMessenger`, and every result carries honest "test physics list" labels.

I trust these pages more than most tutorials I have seen.

## What would stop me as a paying student

1. **There is no environment setup** (week01 · "Prepare for Week 2: check your Geant4 install"). The page says "Source your Geant4 and ROOT environment", and that is all. There is no Dockerfile, devcontainer, conda recipe or dataset guidance, and no time budget for any of it. Week 1 also asks for `root-config`, while the FAQ and README say ROOT is only needed for Week 9. For a course that teaches reproducibility in Week 12, this is the biggest gap. Week 9 is worse: the only route is a CERN CVMFS CentOS 7 view, and `201.mac` is never provided.
2. **There is no download and no repository** (week02 · Create the project, and every lab after it). Each lab is 9–17 collapsible files to copy one at a time, plus incremental diffs such as "Start from week03/: keep…". Week 2's five macros come as one `macros.txt` with `====` separators. I want `git clone` and one tag per week.
3. **The Week 10 field scan fails on my run** (week10 · Lab 10: f against B). `asymmetry.py` seeds every fit with `p0=[0.25, 1.35, …]`, the 10 mT frequency. At 20 mT my fit stuck at f = 1.3528 ± 0.0289 MHz (χ²/ndf 1860/176). `field_scan.py` ignored the WARNING and printed pull −46.99 and a slope of 135.372 with χ²/ndf 2203/3. With the starting value at 2.71 MHz, the same data give the page's 2.7069. The page's success depends on luck in the thread race it describes.
4. **A Week 8 instruction reads as a remark** (week08 · Create the project from Week 5). "Keep Week 5's … PrimaryGeneratorAction … (the generator's commands are now under /week08/gun/)". If I keep the file as told, `daq.mac`'s first command is unknown, and Week 2 itself teaches that the rest of the macro is then skipped.
5. **The Week 7 resolution statistic is fragile** (week07 · Check the integrator). The lab intro promises "±3% relative on σ", yet the page explains a 0.0040 → 0.0031 jump as random scattering. `np.std` is driven by the tails: a bootstrap gives ±11%, and a single event at 5.4% moves it. The toy already uses the central-68% half-width, so the lab should too, with an error printed next to it.

## Smaller things

- **week06 · Scan the production cut.** The expected output squeezes the species onto one line. The real output has one species per line, and `grep -A4` shows a CPU line instead of gamma. Also, `./week06 em` or `./week06 QGSP_BIC` silently runs FTFP_BERT, which is ironic on a page about physics choices that fail "without a sound".
- **week10 · check the field first.** "Field at (0,0,0)" prints the member `fB`, not `GetFieldValue()`, so it cannot catch the mistakes section 10.1 warns about.
- **week03.** The `steps_summary.py` docstring says `run100.mac`, which does not exist, and it is the only script CMake does not copy.
- **week07.** A section is numbered "8.2". `useFringeMap` is a const bool that needs a rebuild, and no fringe macro is shipped. The runs are called "momenta" although they are kinetic energies.
- **week04.** A comment says "~28 MeV at 100 MeV", while the box and the run say ~24.
- **week05.** Quiz Q5 quotes 2032/403, but the lab gives 2053/386. A comment points to "section 6.1". `fMessenger` is never deleted. The printout shows `eff = 0.0004 +/- 0.0000`.
- **primer · muons in rock.** It refers to multiple scattering in "Week 4", but nothing explains it before Week 7, where it drives σp/p. As a programmer I needed one definition (Highland) in the lesson, not only in the toy's box.
- **code/ folders** contain page data (`pion_tracks.json`, `run_c_both_above.json`) and `__pycache__`.
- **week12.** "Get it signed off": by whom, when I study self-paced? `repro_check.sh` guesses the executable with `find -perm`.

## Physics for a non-physicist programmer

The background boxes are well pitched: kinetic energy versus momentum, g/cm², "step = segment", and a sensitive detector as a ledger. Weeks 4 and 5 are the clearest physics teaching I have had. Week 7 is where I needed more: why scattering gives a flat σp/p and why measurement error gives σp/p ∝ p. Week 10's spin physics is explained well enough to follow, but it is a lot for an optional week.

## Time

Including the reruns and variants, my real time per week was 2–3.5 h. That fits in my 6 h a week, but only after the install, which nobody budgets and which I estimate at 2–4 h.

## The one change

Ship a **Dockerfile/devcontainer** (Geant4 11.4 + datasets + pinned Python, and a second image for musrSim 10.7 + ROOT). Put the code in a **git repo with one tag per week and CI** that builds every week and diffs the key output lines against the pages. The course already has the hard part, real reproducible outputs. It just doesn't hand students the environment.

**Price I'd pay:** SGD 150–250 self-paced; SGD 450–650 with TA code review; +150 for an assessed defence.
