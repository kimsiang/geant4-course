# P03 · 王悦 (Wang Yue): pilot feedback

I'm a third-year physics undergraduate at SJTU and I want to apply for HEP PhD programmes. I have done QM, intro particle physics, C++ coursework (classes, no templates) and numpy/matplotlib, and I run Ubuntu on a ThinkPad. I studied all twelve weeks on the physics track, in EN+中 mode, because I want the English terms. I have about 8 hours a week.

**How I actually ran the code.** I did not install Geant4 11.4 myself. The course has no install guide (more on that below), so for this pilot I used the course's compile harness: Geant4 11.4 with a test physics list and no datasets. I built and ran Weeks 2, 3, 4, 5, 8 and 10. I ran the Python parts of Weeks 1, 9 and 11, and the Week 11 dE/dx benchmark with my Week 4 build. I read Weeks 6, 7 and 12 completely but did not build them.

## The verdict

**NPS: 8/10.** The best thing about this course is that it is honest. Every expected output I ran matched to the last printed digit: Week 1's 0.0482 ± 0.0007, Week 4's five "Run complete" blocks and the Bethe table, Week 5's three trigger runs, Week 8's 322 triggered events, and the Week 10 no-spin control (A0 = −0.0036 ± 0.0038). Where my numbers could not match (thread races, random sequences), the page told me so and told me to compare in σ. The explanations of why the 100 MeV and 10 GeV points miss the Bethe curve, and then the Week 11 rerun where they agree within 0.4σ, taught me more about validation than any course I've taken.

What stops me giving a 9 or 10: setup and file logistics, a handful of steps where doing exactly what the text says gives an error, and a capstone with no hand-in process.

## Problems, in the order I hit them

**Setup (blocker).** Week 1's last step, "Prepare for Week 2: check your Geant4 install", only checks `geant4-config --version` and `--check-datasets`. Nowhere does the course say how to *install* Geant4 11.4 with its datasets on Ubuntu, and from a mainland-China campus network the dataset download is slow. I'd budget about 5 hours, which Week 2's "about 2 h" ignores. When I ran with the dataset variables unset, Geant4 aborted with `G4Exception : PART70000 … G4ENSDFSTATEDATA environment variable must be set`. No common-errors box mentions it. The same step also asks for `root-config`, but the README says ROOT isn't needed until Week 9.

**week02 · Hands-on: Lab 2.** The five macros are one copy block ("run1.mac · gun.mac · outward.mac · pion.mac · vis.mac"). I had to split them by hand, and `configure_file` fails if one is missing. Weeks 3, 5 and 6 do the same. Please give one block per file, or a zip of `code/weekNN/`. After that, everything matched. My pion run gave 211 μ⁻ against 205 ± 13, which is fine.

**week03 · Hands-on: Labs 3.1 and 3.2.** `run1.mac` calls `/run/dumpCouples` *before* `/run/beamOn 1`, so it prints an empty "Table of registered couples". That means I could not reproduce the threshold column ("printed with /run/dumpCouples after a run"). The `steps_summary.py` docstring also mentions a `run100.mac` that doesn't exist.

**week05 · Hands-on: Labs 5.1–5.3 (my worst hour).** The page doesn't say where to start, and "ActionInitialization.hh is unchanged from Week 4" made me copy week04/. Its `SteppingAction.cc` stays in `src/`, the CMake glob picks it up, and the build fails with `error: 'class EventAction' has no member named 'AddEdep'`. Once I started from a clean directory, all three runs matched exactly. Also, quiz Q5 quotes "2032 … 403", while the lab and Course-W05-f say 2053 and 386.

**week08 · Hands-on: Labs 8.1 and 8.2.** "Keep Week 5's … PrimaryGeneratorAction … (the generator's commands are now under /week08/gun/)". I kept it, and daq.mac stopped with `COMMAND NOT FOUND </week08/gun/model cos2> … Batch is interrupted!!` and no file. The changed generator isn't shown on the page. With the right file, read_muons.py and parity.py matched perfectly.

**week10 · Hands-on: Lab 10.** The 10 mT run and the no-spin control matched the page. But when I ran `run_scan.sh` and `field_scan.py`, my 20 mT fit failed: `f = 1.4190 ± 0.0482 MHz`, "A0 is consistent with zero: there is no oscillation", then `pull −26.80` and `chi2/ndf = 718.11/3`. The cause is that `asymmetry.py` hard-codes the starting frequency `p0=[…, 1.35, …]`. Refitted from 2.71 MHz, the same file gives 2.7070 ± 0.0020 MHz. Because every two-thread run differs (as the page itself explains), other students will hit this too, and the script will tell them their physics is broken. Seed the fit from the configured field.

**week06 · Practice quiz Q5.** It says the e⁻ threshold in plastic stops changing "below about 10 µm". The course's own `cut_thresholds_g4_11.4.json` gives 14.5 keV at 10 µm. Only the γ threshold reaches the 990 eV floor there. Run A (FTFP_BERT) is only a prediction, so I can't check my π⁻ census.

**week07 · Hands-on: Labs 7.1 and 7.2.** The lab promises "±3% relative on σ". Two runs of the same setup then give 0.0040 and 0.0031 at 1 GeV, and `reconstruct.py` uses `np.std` while the toy uses the central 68%. There is also a stray heading, "## 8.2 From an ideal field…", inside Week 7.

**week09 · Hands-on: Lab 9.** The only build route is CVMFS on CentOS, which a laptop student doesn't have. I did the synthetic-file path, and it matched (τ = 2.1847 ± 0.0270 µs). It would be more honest to say this is *the* path for self-paced students, or to provide a container. The common-errors footer points to "section 11 above"; it should say 9.2.

**week11 · Hands-on: Labs 11.1 and 11.2.** "Instructor builds the fault lab". I don't have an instructor. If I run `make_faults.sh` myself, its comments show me every bug. I also think `cmake --build build` will fail inside faultlab, because the copied `build/` points at the old source directory (I couldn't run CMake to confirm). Lab 11.2 reproduced exactly. Its "naively first" run already uses the path-averaged reference, though, so the 3.7σ in quiz Q4 never shows up in my output.

**week12 · Hands-on: Lab 12 (capstone clarity: 2/5).** The course says "Get it signed off", "your partner asks three questions", "an evaluator clones the repository". As a self-paced student I have none of these people. I don't know where to submit, by when, who grades it, or what a 60 versus an 80 means on each weight. The 4-hour estimate is for something that is really a 3–4 week project. The proposal template and the weights themselves are excellent.

## Learning design

The predict → simulate → compare loop really works. "Think about it" questions answered by a later step are the best part. Difficulty climbs sensibly. Week 5 is the steepest (SD, hits, trigger, digitizer and Birks in one week) and could be split. The Python-to-C++ jump in Week 2 was fine for me, because the code is complete and I only edit lines. Lesson text is clear, and numbered definitions and equations are easy to refer to. The quizzes test understanding, and the explanations taught me something even when I was right. Apart from the three problems above, they are good.

## EN+中 mode

This is what I'd pay for. Each heading, paragraph, quiz option and navigator item appears in both languages, one under the other, and it's comfortable at 1366×768. The Chinese reads naturally. Problems:
- "event" is 事件 in Weeks 2–3 but 事例 everywhere else, even in Week 2's own background box.
- The box labels Definition, Example, Theorem, Background, History and Code microscope stay English in 中文 mode.

## What I want most

**Make each week's code a downloadable folder (or git tag) that builds exactly as it is, and add a Week 0 install page with a dataset check.** Five of my problems above (the Week 0 install gap, macro splitting, the Week 5 leftover files, the Week 8 generator, the Week 11 build dir) would disappear. After that, write a real capstone brief with a submission and review process, and fix the Week 10 fit seed.

**Would I recommend it?** Yes, to any classmate going into experimental HEP. It's the first material I've seen that teaches *trusting* a simulation, not just running one. A fair price for me: 199–399 CNY self-paced, 800–1200 CNY with a WeChat group and Q&A, and up to 2000 CNY with a graded capstone and certificate.
