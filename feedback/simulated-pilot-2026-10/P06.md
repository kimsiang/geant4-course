# P06 · Ahmad Faizal: NDT engineer, Kuala Lumpur, muography track

**Who I am.** I'm 35 and an NDT engineer (UT, RT, eddy current) at an oil-and-gas inspection company. I have an EE degree, use MATLAB daily and some Python, and have never written C++. I work on Windows. I study about 3 h a week in the evenings, on a 13-inch laptop or on my phone on the LRT. My manager wants to know whether muon **scattering** tomography could work for pipes and pressure vessels, and whether this course is worth paying for so that 3 more engineers can take it. I read the course home and the primer, all of Weeks 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 and 12, and skimmed Weeks 4, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 11.

## What convinced me

The rigour is real. In Week 1 I ran `telescope_transmission.py` and got exactly the expected output: `0.0570 / 0.0485 / 0.0482`. I spot-checked the physics: the 0.9° primer scattering angle (Highland gives 0.86°), the 45 mrad deflection in Week 7, the 270 km geomagnetic radius and the 4 × 10⁻⁴ Hz accidental rate in the Week 5 quiz. All of them were right. Every toy states its approximations. The "predict → run → compare in σ" habit, the distinction between conditional transmission, acceptance and efficiency, and "kinetic energy is not momentum" are exactly the discipline our inspection reports need. On my phone the week pages read well. The toys work, the item drawer is clear and code blocks scroll inside their frame.

## What would stop me buying it for three engineers

**1. No install path (blocker).** Week 2 says *"You need Geant4 11.4 and CMake ≥ 3.16 (Week 1, step 7)"*, but Week 1 step 7 only checks versions. No page explains how to install Geant4, and nothing covers Windows. Every engineer in my company uses Windows. The landing page promises *"container provided for Windows"*, and the US$290 tier lists "the container image", but I found no container anywhere. I estimate 4–6 h to get WSL2 plus Geant4 with datasets working. That is two of my study weeks, and I would probably need help.

**2. Scattering muography is described but never simulated.** The primer's table names scattering mode for "containers, reinforced concrete, waste drums", which is close to our use case. Then:
- *primer · 3 Muons in rock*: "multiple Coulomb scattering, Week 4". Week 4 never mentions scattering.
- *week06 · Background*: "For scattering muography, compare multiple-scattering models, because the scattering angle is the signal". This is the most important sentence for me, and no exercise follows it.
- *week07 · For muography projects*: "Most muography telescopes have no magnet…". Yet Week 7's two-tracker line fit is exactly how a scattering tomograph measures in/out angles, and the page never says so.

No lab in `code/` puts a muon through rock or steel, and none samples a cosmic energy spectrum (the generators use fixed energies). For my manager the course teaches Geant4 foundations well, but not our application.

**3. The capstone doesn't fit the muography track or self-study.** Week 12 says *"Week 10's app is a complete template"*. Every Lab 12 example is μSR (`tf.mac`, `repro_check.sh … week10`), and Week 10 is optional for my track. The defence needs *"Your partner asks three questions"* and "peers". Studying alone, I have neither, and there is nowhere to submit.

**4. No certificate or proof of completion.** Progress is "Only in this browser, on this device". It doesn't even sync between my phone and laptop. The company needs a record for training files (an HRD Corp claim would help). E7: I would trust what I've learned if I had a graded capstone and a certificate showing hours.

**5. The time estimates are optimistic for part-timers.** The home page says "2–3 h a week, about 30 hours". The page totals already reach 3 h for Weeks 5, 9 and 10 and 4 h for Week 12, and that excludes install and debugging. For someone new to C++ at 3 h/week, Weeks 2, 5 and 7 are two-week units each. Realistically the course takes 20–24 calendar weeks. The landing page itself says "about five hours a week".

**6. The landing page and the course disagree.** Landing says *"09–10 A multi-layer muon telescope, as used in muography"*, but the real Weeks 9–10 are musrSim/μSR. The prerequisites also differ: landing asks for "basic C++: classes and pointers", while the course says C++ "helps". A muography buyer would feel misled.

## Smaller issues (page · section)

- **week05 · Practice quiz Q5:** "2032 events … 403" vs the lab's run C and Course-W05-f: 2053 and 386.
- **week05 · Run the three configurations:** `eff = 0.1101` for what the text calls "the geometry". Week 1 MC0003 warns against calling that efficiency. An engineer would report "11 % efficiency" and be wrong.
- **week05 · PrimaryGeneratorAction:** "cos2" samples `u^0.25`, but Week 1's "real sky" step uses `u^(1/3)`. The same name refers to two different samplers. A one-line comment would fix it.
- **week07:** a heading reads "8.2 From an ideal field to a measured field map". It should be 7.2.
- **week03 · steps_summary.py:** the docstring says `run100.mac`, which doesn't exist (the macro is run1000.mac).
- **week01 · Lab 1 step 1:** `source ~/g4c/bin/activate` fails in PowerShell. The Week 3 `sed -i` loop is GNU-only. Say "Linux/WSL/macOS" up front.
- **week02 · Create the project:** I have to paste ten files by hand, and the five macros come as one combined block. Offer a zip or a git clone of `code/weekNN`.
- **week11 · Lab 11.1** is "instructor builds the fault lab". Who builds it in self-paced mode?
- **index (phone):** `index.html` and `landing.html` have no `<meta name=viewport>`, so on my 390 px phone the course home renders at 980 px and shrinks. The primer and week pages are fine. ## Price and value

Per engineer, I think fair prices are: self-paced US$150–250, with support US$400–600, and with a verified certificate and graded capstone US$600–800. A company 3-seat pack *with an industrial scattering module* would be worth ~US$1,500–2,000. The landing's US$290 / 590 / 1,900 is reasonable **only** if the container, the support and a certificate are actually delivered.

**NPS (J1): 5.** I'd recommend it to a physicist colleague today. For my company, I'd tell my manager to fund **one** pilot seat now, and the other three once there's an install path, a scattering lab and a certificate.

**The one change I want most:** a scattering-muography module. It would remove the field from Week 7's spectrometer and put a steel plate or pipe wall between the two tracker stacks. Students would measure the deflection-angle width against wall thickness and compare it with Highland. Then they would make a simple PoCA image and estimate the exposure time. Make that the muography capstone template.
