# P05 · Sarah Okafor: pilot feedback on Geant4 2.0

**Who I am:** I'm a medical-physics MSc graduate and clinical trainee in the UK. I've used GATE and TOPAS for proton-therapy dose but have never written raw Geant4 C++. My Python is good and my C++ is basic. I work on a Windows laptop through WSL2 and have about 5 hours a week. I came for transferable Geant4 core skills, not muon physics. I'm on the physics track but skipping μSR.

**What I read:** I read the course home, skimmed the primer, and read Weeks 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 11 in full, including every code file. I skimmed Weeks 1, 7 and 12 and skipped 9–10. I compile-checked the Week 4 and Week 6 apps against Geant4 11.4 with FTFP_BERT, and both link cleanly. I couldn't run them because the harness has no datasets. The Week 5 link failed on `anHCAllocator_G4MT_TLS_`, which I think is a fault in the harness, not the course.

## Overall

**NPS: 6/10.** The core is better than I expected. The step/track/run model, the three geometry layers, the stepping-action → event → run accumulation, the multithreading section and the validation week are all clear, honest and measured on real 11.4 runs. I'd recommend Weeks 2–6, 8 and 11 to colleagues as "Geant4 for people who only know GATE".

Two things stop me going higher:

- **There is no installation help, and nothing at all for Windows.**
- **There is none of the Geant4 machinery a dosimetry user needs.**

## Setup: the real blocker

No page tells me how to install Geant4 11.4. Week 1 only has a check step ("Prepare for Week 2: check your Geant4 install": `geant4-config --version`). Week 2 then says "You need Geant4 11.4 … (Week 1, step 7)", but that step is the same check.

The words "Windows" and "WSL" appear nowhere. The primer asks for "A Linux or macOS terminal". On WSL2 I'd need to:

- build from source with the datasets, or use conda-forge;
- confirm WSLg is present so `/vis/open` works;
- keep my projects in the Linux filesystem;
- open `g4_00.wrl` in Windows ParaView via `\\wsl$`.

I estimate 3–4 hours before Week 2 even starts. Week 1 also asks for `root-config`, although the FAQ says ROOT is only needed for the musrSim weeks.

On top of that, there's no code download. Each week means pasting 6–17 files by hand, so the 40-minute estimate for Lab 2 is fantasy for a basic-C++ student. Week 6 even says "src/EventAction.cc is one line" instead of showing the file, though the file exists in `code/`.

## What's missing for dosimetry users

- **Scoring.** Neither `/score/create/boxMesh` and `/score/quantity/doseDeposit` nor `G4MultiFunctionalDetector` with `G4PSEnergyDeposit`/`G4PSDoseDeposit` appear anywhere, and "dose" never appears at all. Week 4 hand-writes the Edep accumulation, which is good pedagogy, but the built-in route should sit next to it. It's what GATE and TOPAS wrap.
- **Sources.** Only `G4ParticleGun` is taught. There's no GPS and no phase-space sources.
- **Physics-list depth (Week 6).** There's one global cut and no regions (`/run/setCutForRegion`). There are no EM options (option4, Livermore, Penelope), no `G4PhysListFactory` or `PHYSLIST`, and no `/process/em/` commands. QBBC and QGSP_BIC, the usual lists for hadron therapy, aren't mentioned. Course-W06-f even tells you to record "any EM option" without ever showing one.
- **Variance reduction or biasing.** None.
- **Built-in Birks.** Week 5 hand-rolls Birks and never mentions `G4EmSaturation` or `SetBirksConstant`.

## Is the physics-list and cut content correct and current?

Mostly yes. I checked these against the 11.4 source:

- The eight FTFP_BERT constructors are right.
- The 0.7 mm default cut in FTFP_BERT and the 1 mm default in `G4VModularPhysicsList` are right.
- The threshold table and the Edep-stable / track-count-explodes demonstration are excellent.

Problems:

- **Overstated tracking claim.** Week 6 · "Production cuts" says produced particles "are tracked down to zero energy". In fact `lowestElectronEnergy` is 1 keV.
- **Which processes use cuts.** Cuts apply only to ionisation, bremsstrahlung and pair-production-type processes. Photoelectric, Compton and conversion secondaries ignore the cut unless `applyCuts` is on.
- **Week 6 quiz Q5 is wrong by the course's own data.** It says "Below about 10 µm in plastic, lowering the cut no longer changes the e⁻ threshold". But `cut_thresholds_g4_11.4.json` gives 14.5 keV at 10 µm and reaches the 990 eV floor only at 1 µm.
- **Condensed expected output.** The Week 6 lab step 2 output isn't what the program prints. The CPU line is missing, yet the text quotes "12-fold" CPU.

## Other defects

- **Contradiction between Weeks 5 and 11.** Week 5 · Common errors (Course-W05-c) says a misspelt SD volume name means "The SD is never called; every efficiency is 0, with no error". Week 11 and the 11.4 source say it's fatal (Run0053). Fix Week 5.
- **Week 5 quiz Q5** quotes "2032 … 403". The lab output says 2053 and 386.
- **Week 11 fault lab.** The loop `cmake --build build` fails because faultlab was never configured. If `week05/build` exists, `make_faults.sh` copies a stale CMakeCache and commits it.
- **Week 4 numbers.** Course-W04-a says "~24 MeV at 100 MeV", but the RunAction comment says "~28 MeV".
- **Wrong references:**
  - The Week 5 EventAction comment points to "section 6.1"; it should be 5.1.
  - The Week 3 docstring uses `run100.mac`, which doesn't exist.
  - Week 7 has a heading numbered "8.2".
- **Week 12.** The capstone template is "Week 10's app", which I'd skip. Every example topic is a muon topic, and the page never says who the "evaluator" is.
- **Support.** No help channel is named anywhere.

## Best and worst

**Best:** Weeks 6 and 11. Measuring that Edep doesn't move while δ-rays grow 63-fold, and refusing to quote a σ on a truncated mean, are habits I'll take straight into dose validation. Week 8's MT merging, with its observed failure modes, is the clearest explanation I've seen.

**Worst:** I'd be stuck before Week 2 on Windows. And the course ends just before the tools I actually need.

## The one change I want most

Add a setup page with a WSL2 path. Then add a scoring week: scoring meshes, primitive scorers, dose in a water phantom, GPS, regions with per-region cuts, and a comparison of EM options. With those two additions I'd score it 9 and sell it to my department.

**Fair price:** £80–150 self-paced; £250–400 with TA support; £400–600 with a graded capstone and certificate.
