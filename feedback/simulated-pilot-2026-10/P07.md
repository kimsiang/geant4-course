# P07 · 陈静 (Chen Jing): μSR user, physics track

I am a condensed-matter postdoc in Beijing. I get beamtime at PSI and ISIS, I fit with musrfit, and I write Python every day. I have never written C++ and never used Geant4. I took this course because I want to simulate my own sample environment and positron counters: how many muons stop in the holder instead of the sample, what instrument asymmetry I should expect, and what α will be. I studied in 中文 mode and checked the English when a formula looked odd. I can give about 4 hours a week.

**What I read.** I read the course home, the primer and Weeks 1, 2, 8, 9 and 10 completely. I skimmed Weeks 3–7, 11 and 12 (headings, physics boxes and quizzes only).

## First contact
The home page is clear and attractive, and the FAQ is honest about needing two Geant4 versions. But the "从这里开始" block and the whole primer are written for civil engineers ("如果你来自土木工程或地质学…"). Nothing in the primer covers accelerator muons, spin or μSR, even though the physics track ends in μSR. I read it anyway. The glossary was useful, and the rest was not for me. **A physics-track primer would make me feel the course is also mine.**

## Weeks 1–2
Week 1 is excellent. I ran telescope_transmission.py and every number matched to the digit, including the +0.88σ pull. The trap where uniform-θ sampling gives 0.170, the same value as the cos³ crossing model, is clever. The cos³ explanation is hidden in the last paragraph of step 5, though.

Week 2 is where I, with zero C++, slowed down. I had to paste nine files by hand, and the page does not explain `->`, `new` or `override`. The code compiles against 11.4, which I checked with the course harness. The decay-length table is correct: I recomputed βγcτ for all four rows. **Please add a per-week zip download and a tiny "C++ for Python users" box.**

## Week 8
For me this is the most transferable week. Keeping a DAQ tree separate from a truth tree is exactly how our real data and our simulations should meet. The silent failures it shows (no merging, no CloseFile) are good to know about.

## Week 9 (musrSim)
I liked the honesty: the claims about randomOption, the atoi() run number and data/ are checked against the source, and I confirmed them in musrsim-sms. The macro-parser toy is lovely. But:
- **I cannot build musrSim.** The only recipe is a CERN CVMFS LCG_101 centos7 view, and I have no CVMFS in Beijing. The page itself admits that the course never built or ran musrSim either, so every musrSim output on the page comes from reading the source, not from a run.
- **201.mac is never explained.** It is the old GPD instrument macro from musrsim-sms/run/. It needs GPD_coil_primitive.table and vis.mac and ends with `/run/beamOn 10`. production.sh only works from a directory layout that the page never describes.
- analyse_t1.py fits Poisson counts without weights, so the lifetime error it prints is not a valid uncertainty. Week 10 weights its fit correctly.
- The common-errors box says "见上文第 11 节", but the page has no section 11 (the box is in 9.2).

## Week 10 (TF-μSR): why I would pay
I checked the physics carefully, and it is right. γμ/2π = 135.5388 MHz/T, 10 mT gives 1.3554 MHz with T = 0.738 µs, and the sense of precession is correct: dS/dt = γ S×B takes −z toward −y in B∥+x, so D sees the excess first. N_U,D ∝ 1 ∓ A0 sin ωt is right, the toy's a(1+cos α)/2 = 0.287 is right, and the surface-muon 4.1 MeV is right.

I compiled week10 and ran 40 000 muons with the harness test physics. I got A0 = 0.293 ± 0.023 and f = 1.3584 ± 0.0063 MHz, so B = 10.02 ± 0.05 mT. That is consistent with the page within statistics.

The no-spin control is the best idea in the course. Stock FTFP_BERT has no spin-dependent muon decay, and neither does musrsim-sms's main(). I verified this in the source. I would never have guessed it.

What a μSR user still misses:
1. **Positron-energy dependence.** The page only says "a = 1/3 on average, 1 at the endpoint". Please give a(ε) = (2ε−1)/(3−2ε) and say that low-energy positrons have negative asymmetry, so a threshold *raises* A0. Related to this, the toy note's reason "正电子在铜中散射并损失能量" for A0 = 0.24 is incomplete. The real cause is scattering through up to 15 mm of Cu (about one X0) on the way to ±y counters, plus the square slab acceptance.
2. **Sample physics is not in Geant4.** The page should say clearly that the simulation gives the instrument response only. Real Cu has nuclear-dipole damping, and there is no Kubo–Toyabe and no muonium. "λ 与零相容，因为磁场完全均匀" is true only for this idealised sample.
3. **μSR practice is missing:** the α balance parameter, t0, forward/backward and LF/ZF geometries, pulsed ISIS vs continuous PSI beams, and how simulated A0 and α feed into musrfit. An α exercise (move one counter by 2 mm and fit α) would be perfect for me.
4. **The musrSim fix is not really "one line".** musrSim.cc creates FTFP_BERT inline in `SetUserInitialization(new FTFP_BERT(1,steeringFileName))`, so there is no `physicsList` variable to call. Please give the exact 3-line patch with the #include. I checked G4SpinDecayPhysics: its physics type is 0, so RegisterPhysics does add it, and the course's claim is right.
5. Small things: "五个错误中代价最大的" appears under a heading that lists four errors. γμ is never called 旋磁比 / gyromagnetic ratio. "Theorem 10.1" for a physical law reads oddly.

The Chinese μSR terms are good and natural: 横场, 不对称度, 自旋相关衰变, 进动, 表面缪子, 弛豫, 极化.

## Is the ladder reachable for a non-HEP person?
Weeks 1 and 8 are easy for a Python user. Week 2 is the cliff for someone with zero C++, and it is survivable only because every file is given. Week 10 is reachable *in Geant4 11.4*, because the week10 program mirrors tf_musr.mac. The musrSim branch is not reachable for me without a working container.
